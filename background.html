Background

These are notes on the rovibrational theory used by DOI: 10.1063/5.0333947.

Notation

The following typesetting conventions are used:

  • Scalars are in normal font, e.g., \(\Natom\).

  • Tensors (of rank one or higher) are bold, e.g., \(\zetavec\). When a tensor is written in element form the elements are scalars, and are therefore in normal font, e.g., \(\zeta^\alpha_{i,j}\).

  • Operators have hats, e.g., \(\Hvib\). Tensor operators are also bold, e.g., \(\jvec\), but their elements are not, e.g., \(\jop_\alpha\).

  • Anything that depends on the nuclear coordinates or momenta is an operator.

Problem Specification

We assume we have a non-linear molecule with \(\Natom\) atoms. This means that the molecule has \(\Nvib = (3\Natom - 6)\) vibrational degrees of freedom. We will define a molecular reference frame shortly. In the molecular reference frame, \(\rvec\) is the vector containing the Cartesian coordinates of all \(\Natom\) atoms such that \(\rvec_i\) is the position of the \(i\)th atom in the molecule. Similarly, \(\Mvec\) is the diagonal matrix of atomic masses such that \(M_{ii}\) is the mass of the \(i\)th atom.

  • \(\Vop\left(\rvec\right)\) is the potential energy surface (PES) of the molecule.

  • \(\rzvec\) is a minimum energy geometry on the PES. The molecular reference frame is defined so that its origin is the center of mass of \(\rzvec\) and its axes are aligned with the principal axes of inertia of \(\rzvec\).

  • \(\rhovec = \rvec - \rzvec\) is the displacement of the molecule from its minimum energy geometry. Note that \(\rhovec\) satisfy the Eckart conditions.

  • \(\Lvec\) is the the transformation matrix that diagonalizes the Hessian of the potential energy surface at the minimum energy geometry.

  • \(\qvec = \Lvec^T\Mvec^{1/2}\rhovec\) are the mass- weighted normal coordinates of the molecule, which are the vibrational degrees of freedom of the molecule.

  • \(\pvec = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial \qvec}\) are the vibrational momenta of the molecule, which are conjugate to the vibrational degrees of freedom of the molecule.

  • \(\jvec\) is the total angular momentum of the molecule.

  • \(\pivec\) is the vibrational angular momentum of the molecule

  • \(\Ivec\) is the inertia tensor of the molecule with coordinates defined by \(\rvec\).

  • \(\Izvec\) is the inertia tensor of the molecule with coordinates defined by \(\rzvec\).

  • \(\Ieffvec\) is the effective inertia tensor of the molecule with coordinates defined by \(\rvec\).

  • \(\muvec\) is the inverse of the effective inertia tensor of the molecule with coordinates defined by \(\rzvec\).

  • \(\zetavec\) is the Coriolis coupling tensor.

  • \(\gammavec\) are the rotation constants of the molecule.

  • \(\omegavec\) are the harmonic frequencies of the vibrational degrees of freedom of the molecule.

  • \(\Hvib\) is the vibrational Hamiltonian of the molecule.

Definitions

\(\zetavec\) is given by:

\[\zeta_{i,j}^\alpha = \sum_{k=1}^{\Natom} \left(\Lvec_{k,i}\times\Lvec_{k,j}\right)_\alpha\]

\(\Ivec\) is given by:

\[\Iop_{\alpha,\beta} = \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_i \left(\left(\rvec_i\cdot\rvec_i\right)\delta_{\alpha,\beta} - \rop_{i,\alpha}\rop_{i,\beta}\right)\]

\(\Izvec\) is given by:

\[I^0_{\alpha,\alpha} = \frac{\hbar^2}{2\gamma_\alpha}\]

\(\Ieffvec\) is given by:

\[\Ieffop_{\alpha,\beta} = \Iop_{\alpha,\beta} - \sum_{i,j,k}^{\Nvib} \zeta^{\alpha}_{i,k}\zeta^\beta_{j,k} \qop_i \qop_j\]

\(\pivec\) is given by:

\[\piop_\alpha = \sum_{i,j}^{\Nvib} \zeta_{i,j}^\alpha \qop_i \pop_j\]

Deriving the Rovibrational Hamiltonian

\(\Hvib\) is given by:

\[\Hvib = \frac{1}{2}\left(\jvec-\pivec\right)^T\muvec\left(\jvec-\pivec\right) + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\Nvib}\pop_i^2 + \Vop(\qvec) - \frac{\hbar^2}{8}\tr(\muvec)\]

where \(\frac{\hbar^2}{8}\tr(\muvec)\) is the Watson term for a non-linear molecule and is a type of centrifugal distortion term.

\(\Vop(\qvec)\) is then expanded as a Taylor series about the minimum energy geometry (which means the zeroth and 1st order terms are zero). Truncating at the quartic term gives:

\[\begin{split}\Vop(\qvec) \approx + \Vharm + \Vanharm\\ \Vharm \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}^{\Nvib}\omega_i^2 \qop_i^2 \\ \Vanharm \equiv& \frac{1}{6}\sum_{i,j,k}^{\Nvib}g_{i,j,k} \qop_i \qop_j \qop_k + \frac{1}{24}\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}^{\Nvib}F_{i,j,k,\ell} \qop_i \qop_j \qop_k \qop_\ell\end{split}\]

where we used the fact that the harmonic force constants are diagonal in mass-weighted coordinates and defined the third- and fourth-order force constants as \(g_{i,j,k}\) and \(F_{i,j,k,\ell}\) respectively.

For convenience and consistency, \(\muvec\) is also expanded as a fourth-order Taylor series about the minimum energy geometry:

\[\begin{split}\muvec \approx& \sum_{n=0}^{4}\muvec^{(n)}\\ \muvec^{(n)} =& c_n \Izmhalf \bvec^n \Izmhalf\\ c_n =& (n+1)\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^n\\ \bvec =& \sum_{k=1}^{\Nvib} \Izmhalf \avec_k \Izmhalf \qop_k\\ a_{k,\alpha\beta} =& 2 \sum_{\ell=1}^{\Natom} \sum_{m,n=1}^{3} \left(\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{mn} - \delta_{\alpha n}\delta_{\beta m}\right) \sqrt{M_\ell}\, r_{\ell,m}^0 L_{\ell,k,n}\end{split}\]

Inserting the Taylor expansions of \(\Vop(\qvec)\) and \(\muvec\) into the vibrational Hamiltonian, and retaining terms up to fourth order in \(\qvec\) gives:

\[\begin{split}\Hvib =& \HRR + \HHO + \Vanharm + \HvibCor + \Hrovib + \mathcal{O}(\qvec^5)\\ \HHO \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\Nvib}\pop_i^2 + \Vharm\\ \HvibCor\equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{2} \pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\pivec - \sum_{n=0}^{4}\frac{\hbar^2}{8}\tr(\muvec^{(n)})\\ \Hrovib \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{4} \jvec^T\muvec^{(n)}\jvec - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\jvec^T\muvec^{(n)}\pivec- \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\jvec\\ \mathcal{O}(\qvec^5) =& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=3}^{4}\pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\pivec - \frac{1}{2}\jvec^T\muvec^{(4)}\pivec - \frac{1}{2}\pivec^T\muvec^{(4)}\jvec\\\end{split}\]

The rigid rotor Hamiltonian is then defined as:

\[\begin{split}\HRR \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\jvec^T\muvec^{(0)}\jvec\\ =& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3} \jop_i\muop^{(0)}_{i,j}\jop_j\\ =& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3} \frac{\jop_i^2}{I^0_{i,i}}\end{split}\]

Letting \(\alpha = \frac{1}{2I^0_{xx}}\), \(\beta = \frac{1}{2I^0_{yy}}\), and \(\gamma = \frac{1}{2I^0_{zz}}\), we can rewrite

\[\begin{split}\HRR =&\frac{1}{2}\left[\alpha \jop_x^2 + \beta \jop_y^2 + \gamma \jop_z^2\right]\\ =&\frac{\alpha+\beta}{4}\left(\jop_x^2 + \jop_y^2\right) + \frac{\alpha-\beta}{4}\left(\jop_x^2 - \jop_y^2\right) + \frac{\gamma \jop_z^2}{2}\\ =&\frac{\alpha+\beta}{4}\left(\jop^2 - \jop_z^2\right) + \frac{\alpha-\beta}{8}\left(\jop_+^2 + \jop_-^2\right) + \frac{\gamma \jop_z^2}{2}\end{split}\]

The remaining Hamiltonians are easier to work with once \(\muvec^{(n)}\) and \(\pivec\) are written explicitly in terms of \(\qvec\) and \(\pvec\). In the rest of this section \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are Cartesian indices (as in the Definitions section), not the constants used for \(\HRR\). Defining

\[\Bvec_k \equiv \Izmhalf \avec_k \Izmhalf\]

so that \(\bvec = \sum_{k}\Bvec_k \qop_k\), and using the fact that the \(\qop_k\) commute with one another, \(\muvec^{(n)}\) is a polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(\qvec\):

\[\begin{split}\muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta} =& \sum_{\midx}\eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx}\qop^{\midx}\\ \eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx} \equiv& c_n\left[ \Izmhalf \Bvec_{m_1}\Bvec_{m_2}\cdots\Bvec_{m_n} \Izmhalf\right]_{\alpha\beta}\\ \qop^{\midx} \equiv& \qop_{m_1}\qop_{m_2}\cdots \qop_{m_n}\end{split}\]

where \(\midx = \left(m_1, m_2, \ldots, m_n\right)\) is a multi-index and \(\sum_{\midx}\) runs each \(m_t\) from 1 to \(\Nvib\). For \(n=0\), \(\qop^{\midx} = 1\) and \(\eta^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta} = \delta_{\alpha\beta}/I^0_{\alpha\alpha}\). Two properties are used below:

  • \(\avec_k\) is symmetric (a consequence of the Eckart conditions), so \(\muvec^{(n)}\) is symmetric.

  • \(\zeta^\alpha_{i,j} = -\zeta^\alpha_{j,i}\), so \(\zeta^\alpha_{i,i} = 0\) and each \(\piop_\alpha\) is Hermitian.

The vibrational Coriolis Hamiltonian is purely vibrational. Writing out the matrix products and inserting the expansions above:

\[\begin{split}\HvibCor =& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3} \piop_\alpha\muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}\piop_\beta - \frac{\hbar^2}{8}\sum_{n=0}^{4}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3} \muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\alpha}\\ =& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3} \sum_{i,j,k,\ell=1}^{\Nvib}\sum_{\midx} \zeta^\alpha_{i,j}\zeta^\beta_{k,\ell} \eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx} \qop_i \pop_j \qop^{\midx} \qop_k \pop_\ell - \frac{\hbar^2}{8}\sum_{n=0}^{4}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\sum_{\midx} \eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\alpha,\midx}\qop^{\midx}\end{split}\]

The operator ordering must be kept, because \(\pop_j\) does not commute with \(\qop^{\midx}\qop_k\). The \(n=0\) terms reduce to

\[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3} \frac{\piop_\alpha^2}{I^0_{\alpha\alpha}} - \frac{\hbar^2}{8}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3} \frac{1}{I^0_{\alpha\alpha}}\]

the second of which is a constant energy shift.

For the rovibrational coupling Hamiltonian, note that the molecule-fixed components of \(\jvec\) act only on the rotational coordinates, so they commute with \(\qvec\), \(\pvec\), and therefore \(\muvec^{(n)}\) and \(\pivec\). Combined with the symmetry of \(\muvec^{(n)}\), this lets the two Coriolis terms be combined:

\[\begin{split}\pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\jvec =& \sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3} \piop_\alpha\muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}\jop_\beta = \sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3} \jop_\alpha\piop_\beta\muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}\\ \jvec^T\muvec^{(n)}\pivec + \pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\jvec =& \sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3}\jop_\alpha\left( \muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}\piop_\beta + \piop_\beta\muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}\right)\end{split}\]

where the first line relabeled \(\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\). Inserting this and the expansions above:

\[\begin{split}\Hrovib =& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{4}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3} \muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}\jop_\alpha \jop_\beta - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3}\jop_\alpha\left( \muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}\piop_\beta + \piop_\beta\muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}\right)\\ =& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{4}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3}\sum_{\midx} \eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx}\qop^{\midx}\jop_\alpha \jop_\beta\\ &- \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3} \sum_{k,\ell=1}^{\Nvib}\sum_{\midx} \zeta^\beta_{k,\ell}\eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx} \left(\qop^{\midx}\qop_k \pop_\ell + \qop_k \pop_\ell \qop^{\midx}\right) \jop_\alpha\end{split}\]

Because \(\muvec^{(0)}\) is a diagonal constant, the \(n=0\) Coriolis term reduces to

\[-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3} \frac{\jop_\alpha\piop_\alpha}{I^0_{\alpha\alpha}}\]

Matrix Elements

Let \(\ket{v}\) be an eigenfunction of \(\HHO\) with eigenvalue \(E_v=\hbar\omega_v\) and \(\ket{J,K}\) be an eigenfunction of \(\jop^2\) and \(\jop_z\) with eigenvalues \(\hbar^2 J(J+1)\) and \(K\hbar\) respectively (here \(K\) is the projection of \(\jvec\) on to the molecule’s z-axis). A basis for the rovibrational Hamiltonian is then given by the direct product of one \(\ket{v}\) per vibrational degree of freedom and \(\ket{J,K}\), i.e., \(\ket{\vvec,J,K}\).

\(\HRR\) is not diagonal in this basis, because the rigid rotor eigenstates are linear combinations of the \(\ket{J,K}\) states. The matrix elements of \(\HRR\) in this basis are given by:

\[\begin{split}\braket{\vvec',J',K'\mid \HRR\mid\vvec,J,K} =& \frac{1}{2}\delta_{\vvec',\vvec} \braket{J', K'\mid\frac{\alpha+\beta}{4}\left(\jop^2 - \jop_z^2\right) + \frac{\alpha-\beta}{8}\left(\jop_+^2 + \jop_-^2\right) + \frac{\gamma \jop_z^2}{2}\mid J, K}\\\end{split}\]

\(\HHO\) is diagonal in this basis with matrix elements:

\[\bra{\vvec',K'}\HHO\ket{\vvec,K} = \delta_{\vvec',\vvec}\delta_{K',K} \sum_{i=1}^{\Nvib}\hbar\omega_{v_i}\]

\(\HvibCor\) and \(\Hrovib\) are sums of products of \(\qop_k\), \(\pop_k\), and \(\jop_\alpha\), so their matrix elements follow from those of the individual operators.

In mass-weighted normal coordinates \(\qop_k = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_k}} \left(\aop_k + \aop_k^\dagger\right)\) and \(\pop_k = i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}} \left(\aop_k^\dagger - \aop_k\right)\), where \(\aop_k\) and \(\aop_k^\dagger\) are the ladder operators of mode \(k\). The single-mode matrix elements are therefore:

\[\begin{split}\braket{v'_k\mid \qop_k\mid v_k} =& \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_k}} \left(\sqrt{v_k+1}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k+1} + \sqrt{v_k}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k-1}\right)\\ \braket{v'_k\mid \pop_k\mid v_k} =& i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}} \left(\sqrt{v_k+1}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k+1} - \sqrt{v_k}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k-1}\right)\end{split}\]

Operators on different modes commute, so the matrix element of a product of \(\qop\) and \(\pop\) operators factors into one matrix element per mode:

\[\braket{\vvec'\mid \op{O}_1 \op{O}_2\cdots \op{O}_p\mid\vvec} = \prod_{k=1}^{\Nvib}\braket{v'_k\mid \op{O}^{(k)}\mid v_k}\]

where \(\op{O}^{(k)}\) is the product, in the original order, of the factors that act on mode \(k\) (the identity if there are none). Single-mode products are evaluated by inserting \(\sum_{v''_k}\ket{v''_k}\bra{v''_k}\) between factors. Each factor changes one quantum number by \(\pm 1\), so a product of \(p\) factors only connects states with \(\sum_k\left|v'_k - v_k\right| \le p\) and \(\sum_k\left(v'_k - v_k\right) \equiv p \pmod 2\).

The molecule-fixed components of \(\jvec\) obey the anomalous commutation relation \(\left[\jop_x, \jop_y\right] = -i\hbar \jop_z\). As a result \(\jop_\pm = \jop_x \pm i\jop_y\) lower and raise \(K\) respectively (the reverse of the space-fixed case):

\[\jop_\pm\ket{J,K} = \hbar C^\mp_{J,K}\ket{J,K\mp1},\qquad C^\pm_{J,K} \equiv \sqrt{J(J+1) - K(K\pm1)}\]

This does not affect \(\HRR\), which only contains \(\jop_+^2 + \jop_-^2\), but it fixes the signs below. Using \(\jop_x = \frac{1}{2}\left(\jop_+ + \jop_-\right)\) and \(\jop_y = \frac{1}{2i}\left(\jop_+ - \jop_-\right)\):

\[\begin{split}\braket{J',K'\mid \jop_x\mid J,K} =& \frac{\hbar}{2}\delta_{J',J} \left(C^+_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K+1} + C^-_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K-1}\right)\\ \braket{J',K'\mid \jop_y\mid J,K} =& \frac{i\hbar}{2}\delta_{J',J} \left(C^+_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K+1} - C^-_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K-1}\right)\\ \braket{J',K'\mid \jop_z\mid J,K} =& \hbar K\,\delta_{J',J}\delta_{K',K}\end{split}\]

Every \(\jop_\alpha\) conserves \(J\), so products are evaluated within a single \(J\) block:

\[\braket{J,K'\mid \jop_\alpha \jop_\beta\mid J,K} = \sum_{K''=K-1}^{K+1}\braket{J,K'\mid \jop_\alpha\mid J,K''} \braket{J,K''\mid \jop_\beta\mid J,K}\]

which is nonzero only for \(\left|K'-K\right| \le 2\).

\(\HvibCor\) is purely vibrational, so it is diagonal in \(J\) and \(K\):

\[\begin{split}\braket{\vvec',J',K'\mid \HvibCor\mid\vvec,J,K} =& \delta_{J',J}\delta_{K',K}\left[ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3} \sum_{i,j,k,\ell=1}^{\Nvib}\sum_{\midx} \zeta^\alpha_{i,j}\zeta^\beta_{k,\ell} \eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx} \braket{\vvec'\mid \qop_i \pop_j \qop^{\midx} \qop_k \pop_\ell \mid\vvec}\right.\\ &\left.- \frac{\hbar^2}{8}\sum_{n=0}^{4}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3} \sum_{\midx}\eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\alpha,\midx} \braket{\vvec'\mid \qop^{\midx}\mid\vvec}\right]\end{split}\]

The \(n\)th Coriolis term is a product of \(n+4\) vibrational operators and the \(n\)th Watson term is a product of \(n\), which sets the range of \(\vvec'\) each term couples to.

\(\Hrovib\) couples vibration and rotation, and its matrix elements factor into a vibrational and a rotational part:

\[\begin{split}\braket{\vvec',J',K'\mid \Hrovib\mid\vvec,J,K} =& \delta_{J',J}\left[ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{4}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3}\sum_{\midx} \eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx} \braket{\vvec'\mid \qop^{\midx}\mid\vvec} \braket{J,K'\mid \jop_\alpha \jop_\beta\mid J,K}\right.\\ &- \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{3} \sum_{k,\ell=1}^{\Nvib}\sum_{\midx} \zeta^\beta_{k,\ell}\eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx}\\ &\left.\times\braket{\vvec'\mid \qop^{\midx}\qop_k \pop_\ell + \qop_k \pop_\ell \qop^{\midx} \mid\vvec} \braket{J,K'\mid \jop_\alpha\mid J,K}\right]\end{split}\]

The first (centrifugal distortion) term couples \(K\) to \(K, K\pm1, K\pm2\). The second (Coriolis) term couples \(K\) to \(K, K\pm1\).
