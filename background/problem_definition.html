Problem Specification

We assume we have a non-linear molecule with \(\Natom\) atoms. This means that the molecule has \(\Nvib = (3\Natom - 6)\) vibrational degrees of freedom. We will define a molecular reference frame shortly. In the molecular reference frame, \(\rvec\) is the vector containing the Cartesian coordinates of all \(\Natom\) atoms such that \(\rvec_i\) is the position of the \(i\)th atom in the molecule. Similarly, \(\Mvec\) is the diagonal matrix of atomic masses such that \(M_{ii}\) is the mass of the \(i\)th atom. \(\Ivec\) is the inertia tensor of the molecule with coordinates defined by \(\rvec\).

We assume that \(\Vop\left(\rvec\right)\) is the potential energy surface (PES) of the molecule and that \(\rzvec\) is a minimum energy geometry on the PES. The molecular reference frame is defined so that its origin is the center of mass of \(\rzvec\) and its axes are aligned with the principal axes of inertia of \(\rzvec\). To satisfy the Eckart conditions, we will use displacements from \(\rzvec\) to define the geometry of the molecule instead of \(\rvec\). The displacements are defined as \(\rhovec = \rvec - \rzvec\).

The orientation and location of the molecular reference frame with respect to the space-fixed reference frame can be fixed by requiring the center of mass to be at the origin of the space-fixed reference frame and the Hessian of the PES at \(\rzvec\) to be diagonal in mass-weighted Cartesian coordinates. \(\Lvec\) is the transformation matrix that diagonalizes the Hessian and \(\Izvec\) is the diagonal inertia tensor of the molecule at \(\rzvec\). \(\qvec = \Lvec^T\Mvec^{1/2}\rhovec\) are the resulting mass-weighted normal coordinates of the molecule describing the vibrational degrees of freedom. The corresponding vibrational momenta are \(\pvec = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial \qvec}\). As the molecule vibrates and rotations it will be assumed to have total angular momentum \(\jvec\) and vibrational angular momentum \(\pivec\).

Other quantities used in the rovibrational Hamiltonian are:

  • \(\Ieffvec\) is the effective inertia tensor of the molecule with coordinates defined by \(\rvec\); it is the difference between \(\Ivec\) and the vibrational contribution to the inertia tensor.

  • \(\muvec\) is the inverse of the effective inertia tensor of the molecule with coordinates defined by \(\rzvec\).

  • \(\zetavec\) is the Coriolis coupling tensor.

  • \(\gammavec\) are the rotation constants of the molecule.

  • \(\omegavec\) are the harmonic frequencies of the vibrational degrees of freedom of the molecule.

  • \(\Hvib\) is the vibrational Hamiltonian of the molecule.

Definitions

\(\zetavec\) is given by:

\[\zeta_{i,j}^\alpha = \sum_{k=1}^{\Natom} \left(\Lvec_{k,i}\times\Lvec_{k,j}\right)_\alpha\]

\(\Ivec\) is given by:

\[\Iop_{\alpha,\beta} = \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_i \left(\left(\rvec_i\cdot\rvec_i\right)\delta_{\alpha,\beta} - \rop_{i,\alpha}\rop_{i,\beta}\right)\]

\(\Izvec\) is given by:

\[I^0_{\alpha,\alpha} = \frac{\hbar^2}{2\gamma_\alpha}\]

\(\Ieffvec\) is given by:

\[\Ieffop_{\alpha,\beta} = \Iop_{\alpha,\beta} - \sum_{i,j,k}^{\Nvib} \zeta^{\alpha}_{i,k}\zeta^\beta_{j,k} \qop_i \qop_j\]

\(\pivec\) is given by:

\[\piop_\alpha = \sum_{i,j}^{\Nvib} \zeta_{i,j}^\alpha \qop_i \pop_j\]

Deriving the Rovibrational Hamiltonian

\(\Hvib\) is given by:

\[\Hvib = \frac{1}{2}\left(\jvec-\pivec\right)^T\muvec\left(\jvec-\pivec\right) + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\Nvib}\pop_i^2 + \Vop(\qvec) - \frac{\hbar^2}{8}\tr(\muvec)\]

where \(\frac{\hbar^2}{8}\tr(\muvec)\) is the Watson term for a non-linear molecule and is a type of centrifugal distortion term.

\(\Vop(\qvec)\) is then expanded as a Taylor series about the minimum energy geometry (which means the zeroth and 1st order terms are zero). Truncating at the quartic term gives:

\[\begin{split}\Vop(\qvec) \approx& \Vharm + \Vanharm\\ \Vharm \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}^{\Nvib}\omega_i^2 \qop_i^2 \\ \Vanharm \equiv& \frac{1}{6}\sum_{i,j,k}^{\Nvib}g_{i,j,k} \qop_i \qop_j \qop_k + \frac{1}{24}\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}^{\Nvib}F_{i,j,k,\ell} \qop_i \qop_j \qop_k \qop_\ell\end{split}\]

where we used the fact that the harmonic force constants are diagonal in mass-weighted coordinates and defined the third- and fourth-order force constants as \(g_{i,j,k}\) and \(F_{i,j,k,\ell}\) respectively.

For convenience and consistency, \(\muvec\) is also expanded as a fourth-order Taylor series about the minimum energy geometry:

\[\begin{split}\muvec \approx& \sum_{n=0}^{4}\muvec^{(n)}\\ \muvec^{(n)} =& c_n \Izmhalf \bvec^n \Izmhalf\\ c_n =& (n+1)\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^n\\ \bvec =& \sum_{k=1}^{\Nvib} \Izmhalf \avec_k \Izmhalf \qop_k\\ a_{k,\alpha\beta} =& 2 \sum_{\ell=1}^{\Natom} \sum_{m,n=1}^{3} \left(\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{mn} - \delta_{\alpha n}\delta_{\beta m}\right) \sqrt{M_\ell}\, r_{\ell,m}^0 L_{\ell,k,n}\end{split}\]

Inserting the Taylor expansions of \(\Vop(\qvec)\) and \(\muvec\) into the vibrational Hamiltonian, and retaining terms up to fourth order in \(\qvec\) gives:

\[\begin{split}\Hvib =& \HRR + \HHO + \Vanharm + \HvibCor + \Hrovib + \Hwatson + \mathcal{O}(\qvec^5)\\ \HHO \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\Nvib}\pop_i^2 + \Vharm\\ \HvibCor\equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{2} \pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\pivec\\ \Hwatson \equiv& -\sum_{n=0}^{4}\frac{\hbar^2}{8}\tr(\muvec^{(n)})\\ \Hrovib \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{4} \jvec^T\muvec^{(n)}\jvec - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\jvec^T\muvec^{(n)}\pivec- \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\jvec\\ \mathcal{O}(\qvec^5) =& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=3}^{4}\pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\pivec - \frac{1}{2}\jvec^T\muvec^{(4)}\pivec - \frac{1}{2}\pivec^T\muvec^{(4)}\jvec\\\end{split}\]

  • See Harmonic Oscillator Hamiltonian for details on the harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian, \(\HHO\).

  • See Vibrational-Coriolis Hamiltonian for details on the vibrational Coriolis Hamiltonian, \(\HvibCor\).

  • See Watson Hamiltonian for details on the Watson Hamiltonian, \(\Hwatson\).

The remaining Hamiltonians are easier to work with once \(\muvec^{(n)}\) and \(\pivec\) are written explicitly in terms of \(\qvec\) and \(\pvec\).

\[\Bvec_k \equiv \Izmhalf \avec_k \Izmhalf\]

so that \(\bvec = \sum_{k}\Bvec_k \qop_k\), and using the fact that the \(\qop_k\) commute with one another, \(\muvec^{(n)}\) is a polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(\qvec\):

\[\begin{split}\muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta} =& \sum_{\midx}\eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx}\qop^{\midx}\\ \eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx} \equiv& c_n\left[ \Izmhalf \Bvec_{m_1}\Bvec_{m_2}\cdots\Bvec_{m_n} \Izmhalf\right]_{\alpha\beta}\\ \qop^{\midx} \equiv& \qop_{m_1}\qop_{m_2}\cdots \qop_{m_n}\end{split}\]

where \(\midx = \left(m_1, m_2, \ldots, m_n\right)\) is a multi-index and \(\sum_{\midx}\) runs each \(m_t\) from 1 to \(\Nvib\). For \(n=0\), \(\qop^{\midx} = 1\) and \(\eta^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta} = \delta_{\alpha\beta}/I^0_{\alpha\alpha}\). Two properties are used below:

  • \(\avec_k\) is symmetric (a consequence of the Eckart conditions), so \(\muvec^{(n)}\) is symmetric.

  • \(\zeta^\alpha_{i,j} = -\zeta^\alpha_{j,i}\), so \(\zeta^\alpha_{i,i} = 0\) and each \(\piop_\alpha\) is Hermitian.

Matrix Elements

Let \(\ket{v}\) be an eigenfunction of \(\HHO\) with eigenvalue \(E_v=\hbar\omega\left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right)\) and \(\ket{J,K}\) be an eigenfunction of \(\jop^2\) and \(\jop_z\) with eigenvalues \(\hbar^2 J(J+1)\) and \(K\hbar\) respectively (here \(K\) is the projection of \(\jvec\) on to the molecule’s z-axis). A basis for the rovibrational Hamiltonian is then given by the direct product of one \(\ket{v}\) per vibrational degree of freedom and \(\ket{J,K}\), i.e., \(\ket{\vvec,J,K}\).

The molecule-fixed components of \(\jvec\) obey the anomalous commutation relation \(\left[\jop_x, \jop_y\right] = -i\hbar \jop_z\). As a result \(\jop_\pm = \jop_x \pm i\jop_y\) lower and raise \(K\) respectively (the reverse of the space-fixed case):

\[\jop_\pm\ket{J,K} = \hbar C^\mp_{J,K}\ket{J,K\mp1},\qquad C^\pm_{J,K} \equiv \sqrt{J(J+1) - K(K\pm1)}\]

\(\HvibCor\) and \(\Hrovib\) are sums of products of \(\qop_k\), \(\pop_k\), and \(\jop_\alpha\), so their matrix elements follow from those of the individual operators.

In mass-weighted normal coordinates \(\qop_k = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_k}} \left(\aop_k + \aop_k^\dagger\right)\) and \(\pop_k = i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}} \left(\aop_k^\dagger - \aop_k\right)\), where \(\aop_k\) and \(\aop_k^\dagger\) are the ladder operators of mode \(k\). The single-mode matrix elements are therefore:

\[\begin{split}\braket{v'_k\mid \qop_k\mid v_k} =& \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_k}} \left(\sqrt{v_k+1}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k+1} + \sqrt{v_k}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k-1}\right)\\ \braket{v'_k\mid \pop_k\mid v_k} =& i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}} \left(\sqrt{v_k+1}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k+1} - \sqrt{v_k}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k-1}\right)\end{split}\]

Operators on different modes commute, so the matrix element of a product of \(\qop\) and \(\pop\) operators factors into one matrix element per mode:

\[\braket{\vvec'\mid \op{O}_1 \op{O}_2\cdots \op{O}_p\mid\vvec} = \prod_{k=1}^{\Nvib}\braket{v'_k\mid \op{O}^{(k)}\mid v_k}\]

where \(\op{O}^{(k)}\) is the product, in the original order, of the factors that act on mode \(k\) (the identity if there are none). Single-mode products are evaluated by inserting \(\sum_{v''_k}\ket{v''_k}\bra{v''_k}\) between factors. Each factor changes one quantum number by \(\pm 1\), so a product of \(p\) factors only connects states with \(\sum_k\left|v'_k - v_k\right| \le p\) and \(\sum_k\left(v'_k - v_k\right) \equiv p \pmod 2\).

The reversed action of \(\jop_\pm\) does not change the magnitude of the \(\HRR\) matrix elements, which only involve \(\jop_+^2 + \jop_-^2\), but it fixes the signs below. Using \(\jop_x = \frac{1}{2}\left(\jop_+ + \jop_-\right)\) and \(\jop_y = \frac{1}{2i}\left(\jop_+ - \jop_-\right)\):

\[\begin{split}\braket{J',K'\mid \jop_x\mid J,K} =& \frac{\hbar}{2}\delta_{J',J} \left(C^+_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K+1} + C^-_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K-1}\right)\\ \braket{J',K'\mid \jop_y\mid J,K} =& \frac{i\hbar}{2}\delta_{J',J} \left(C^+_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K+1} - C^-_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K-1}\right)\\ \braket{J',K'\mid \jop_z\mid J,K} =& \hbar K\,\delta_{J',J}\delta_{K',K}\end{split}\]

Every \(\jop_\alpha\) conserves \(J\), so products are evaluated within a single \(J\) block:

\[\braket{J,K'\mid \jop_\alpha \jop_\beta\mid J,K} = \sum_{K''=K-1}^{K+1}\braket{J,K'\mid \jop_\alpha\mid J,K''} \braket{J,K''\mid \jop_\beta\mid J,K}\]

which is nonzero only for \(\left|K'-K\right| \le 2\).
