Problem Specification

The goal of rovib is to model the rovibrational energy levels of a non-linear molecule containing \(\Natom\) atoms. The location of the \(i\)th atom in the molecule is given by the vector \(\vec{r}_i\) and the location of the molecule as a whole is given by the matrix of atomic coordinates, \(\tens{r} = \left({\vec{r}}_1, {\vec{r}}_2, \ldots, {\vec{r}}_{\Natom}\right)\). Similarly, \(\Mvec\) is the diagonal matrix of atomic masses such that \(M_{ii}\) is the mass of the \(i\)th atom. We assume that the Born-Oppenheimer approximation is valid. In this case, we can assume that the nuclei move on a single potential energy surface (PES), \(V\left(\tens{r}\right)\), defined by the electronic Hamiltonian. We will also assume that we have located a minimum energy geometry on the PES, \(\rzvec\). As the molecule vibrates and rotations it will be assumed to have total angular momentum \(\tens{J}\).

Our original goal can be restated as solving the time-independent Schrödinger equation for the motion of the nuclei on the PES.

Coordinate System

Main article: Coordinate System.

If \(\tens{r}\) and \(\tens{r}^0\) are defined in the Eckart frame, then the internal coordinates of the molecule are given by:

\[\tens{Q} = \Lvec^T\Mvec^{1/2}\left(\tens{r} - \tens{r}^0\right)\]

where \(\Lvec\) is the transformation matrix that diagonalizes the mass- weighted Hessian of the PES at \(\tens{r}^0\). The corresponding vibrational momenta are given by: \(\tens{P} = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial \tens{Q}}\).

In practice, we will solve the rovibrational Schrödinger equation using second quantization. In this basis, the vibrational coordinates and momenta take on the form of creation and annihilation operators, \(\qvec\) and \(\pvec\), and the coordinate forms of \(\tens{Q}\) and \(\tens{P}\) are needed only to compute the necessary coefficients for the second-quantized Hamiltonian.

Defining the Rovibrational Hamiltonian

The total rovibrational Hamiltonian \(\Hvib\) of the molecule is given by:

\[\Hvib = \frac{1}{2}\left(\jvec-\pivec\right)^T\muvec\left(\jvec-\pivec\right) + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\Nvib}\pop_i^2 + \Vop(\qvec) - \frac{\hbar^2}{8}\tr(\muvec)\]

Here \(\pivec\) is the angular momentum of the atoms due to their vibrational motion, it is given by:

\[\piop_\alpha = \sum_{i,j}^{\Nvib} \zeta_{i,j}^\alpha \qop_i \pop_j\]

where \(\zetavec\) is the Coriolis coupling constants. \(\zetavec\) is given by:

\[\zeta_{i,j}^\alpha = \sum_{k=1}^{\Natom} \left(\Lvec_{ki}\times\Lvec_{kj}\right)_\alpha.\]

\(\muvec\) is the inverse of the effective inertia tensor, \(\Ieffvec\). \(\Ieffvec\) is given by:

\[\Ieffop_{\alpha,\beta} = \Iop_{\alpha,\beta} - \sum_{i,j,k}^{\Nvib} \zeta^{\alpha}_{i,k}\zeta^\beta_{j,k} \qop_i \qop_j\]

where \(\Ivec\) is the instantaneous inertia tensor of the molecule.

In practice, we rarely have an analytical form of the PES, so we instead Taylor expand \(\Vop(\qvec)\) in displacements from the minimum energy geometry. We set the energy of the minimum energy geometry to zero (so the zeroth term of the Taylor series is zero). Similarly, since the expansion point is the minimum energy geometry the gradient is zero (which means 1st order term is also zero). Truncating the series at the quartic term gives:

\[\begin{split}\Vop(\qvec) \approx& \Vharm + \Vanharm\\ \Vharm \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}^{\Nvib}\omega_i^2 \qop_i^2 \\ \Vanharm \equiv& \frac{1}{6}\sum_{i,j,k}^{\Nvib}g_{i,j,k} \qop_i \qop_j \qop_k + \frac{1}{24}\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}^{\Nvib}F_{i,j,k,\ell} \qop_i \qop_j \qop_k \qop_\ell\end{split}\]

where we used the fact that the harmonic force constants are diagonal in the normal coordinate basis and introduced the third- and fourth-order force constants as \(g_{i,j,k}\) and \(F_{i,j,k,\ell}\) respectively.

Similarly, since \(\muvec\) is also a function of the normal coordinates, it is also expanded as a fourth-order Taylor series about the minimum energy geometry, \(\muvec \approx \sum_{n=0}^{4}\muvec^{(n)}\).

Inserting the Taylor expansions of \(\Vop(\qvec)\) and \(\muvec\) into the vibrational Hamiltonian, and retaining terms up to fourth order in \(\qvec\) gives:

\[\Hvib =& \HHO + \HRR + \Vanharm + \HvibCor + \Hrovib + \Hwatson + \mathcal{O}(\qvec^5)\]

Where:

  • \(\HHO \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\Nvib}\pop_i^2 + \Vharm\) is the harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian.

  • \(\HRR \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\jvec^T\muvec^{(0)}\jvec\) is the rigid rotor Hamiltonian.

  • \(\Vanharm\) is the anharmonic potential energy.

  • \(\HvibCor\equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\pivec\) is the vibrational Coriolis Hamiltonian.

  • \(\Hwatson \equiv& -\sum_{n=0}^{4}\frac{\hbar^2}{8}\tr(\muvec^{(n)})\) is

    the Watson Hamiltonian.

and \(\Hrovib\) is the rovibrational coupling Hamiltonian, which is given by:

\[\Hrovib \equiv& \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{4}\jvec^T\muvec^{(n)}\jvec - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\jvec^T\muvec^{(n)}\pivec- \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\pivec^T\muvec^{(n)}\jvec\]

Matrix Elements of the Hamiltonian

The rovibtrational problem is solved by diagonalizing \(\Hvib\) in a basis set \(\ket{\vvec,J,K}\). The basis functions are the direct product of \(\Nvib\) harmonic oscillator eigenfunctions and an angular momentum eigenfunction.

Harmonic Oscillator Matrix Elements

The Harmonic Oscillator Hamiltonian, \(\HHO\), is diagonal in the \(\ket{\vvec,J,K}\) basis with matrix elements:

\[\braket{\vvec',J',K'\mid\HHO\mid\vvec,J,K} = \delta_{\vvec',\vvec}\delta_{J',J}\delta_{K',K} \sum_{i=1}^{\Nvib}\hbar\omega_i\left(v_i + \frac{1}{2}\right)\]

Rigid Rotor Matrix Elements

Main article: Rigid Rotor Hamiltonian.

In the \(\ket{\vvec,J,K}\) basis, the rigid rotor Hamiltonian, \(\HRR\), is diagonal in \(\vvec\) and \(J\), but not \(K\). The matrix elements are:

\[\braket{\vvec',J',K'\mid \HRR\mid\vvec,J,K} = \delta_{\vvec',\vvec}\delta_{J',J}\left( T^{(\text{RR},0)}_{J,K',K} + T^{(\text{RR},-2)}_{J,K',K} + T^{(\text{RR},2)}_{J,K',K}\right)\]

where:

\[\begin{split}T^{(\text{RR},0)}_{J,K',K} \equiv& \delta_{K',K}\left[ \frac{\gamma_x+\gamma_y}{2} \left(J(J+1) - K^2\right) + \gamma_z K^2\right]\\ T^{(\text{RR},-2)}_{J,K',K} \equiv& \delta_{K',K-2} \frac{\gamma_x-\gamma_y}{4} C^-_{J,K}C^-_{J,K-1}\\ T^{(\text{RR},2)}_{J,K',K} \equiv& \delta_{K',K+2} \frac{\gamma_x-\gamma_y}{4} C^+_{J,K}C^+_{J,K+1}\end{split}\]

with \(\gamma_x\), \(\gamma_y\), and \(\gamma_z\) being the rotational constants of the molecule and \(C^\pm_{J,K} =& \sqrt{J(J+1) - K(K\pm1)}\).

\[\Bvec_k \equiv \Izmhalf \avec_k \Izmhalf\]

so that \(\bvec = \sum_{k}\Bvec_k \qop_k\), and using the fact that the \(\qop_k\) commute with one another, \(\muvec^{(n)}\) is a polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(\qvec\):

\[\begin{split}\muop^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta} =& \sum_{\midx}\eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx}\qop^{\midx}\\ \eta^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta,\midx} \equiv& c_n\left[ \Izmhalf \Bvec_{m_1}\Bvec_{m_2}\cdots\Bvec_{m_n} \Izmhalf\right]_{\alpha\beta}\\ \qop^{\midx} \equiv& \qop_{m_1}\qop_{m_2}\cdots \qop_{m_n}\end{split}\]

where \(\midx = \left(m_1, m_2, \ldots, m_n\right)\) is a multi-index and \(\sum_{\midx}\) runs each \(m_t\) from 1 to \(\Nvib\). For \(n=0\), \(\qop^{\midx} = 1\) and \(\eta^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta} = \delta_{\alpha\beta}/I^0_{\alpha\alpha}\). Two properties are used below:

  • \(\avec_k\) is symmetric (a consequence of the Eckart conditions), so \(\muvec^{(n)}\) is symmetric.

  • \(\zeta^\alpha_{i,j} = -\zeta^\alpha_{j,i}\), so \(\zeta^\alpha_{i,i} = 0\) and each \(\piop_\alpha\) is Hermitian.

Matrix Elements

Let \(\ket{v}\) be an eigenfunction of \(\HHO\) with eigenvalue \(E_v=\hbar\omega\left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right)\) and \(\ket{J,K}\) be an eigenfunction of \(\jop^2\) and \(\jop_z\) with eigenvalues \(\hbar^2 J(J+1)\) and \(K\hbar\) respectively (here \(K\) is the projection of \(\jvec\) on to the molecule’s z-axis). A basis for the rovibrational Hamiltonian is then given by the direct product of one \(\ket{v}\) per vibrational degree of freedom and \(\ket{J,K}\), i.e., \(\ket{\vvec,J,K}\).

\(\HvibCor\) and \(\Hrovib\) are sums of products of \(\qop_k\), \(\pop_k\), and \(\jop_\alpha\), so their matrix elements follow from those of the individual operators.

In mass-weighted normal coordinates \(\qop_k = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_k}} \left(\aop_k + \aop_k^\dagger\right)\) and \(\pop_k = i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}} \left(\aop_k^\dagger - \aop_k\right)\), where \(\aop_k\) and \(\aop_k^\dagger\) are the ladder operators of mode \(k\). The single-mode matrix elements are therefore:

\[\begin{split}\braket{v'_k\mid \qop_k\mid v_k} =& \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_k}} \left(\sqrt{v_k+1}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k+1} + \sqrt{v_k}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k-1}\right)\\ \braket{v'_k\mid \pop_k\mid v_k} =& i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}} \left(\sqrt{v_k+1}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k+1} - \sqrt{v_k}\,\delta_{v'_k,v_k-1}\right)\end{split}\]

Operators on different modes commute, so the matrix element of a product of \(\qop\) and \(\pop\) operators factors into one matrix element per mode:

\[\braket{\vvec'\mid \op{O}_1 \op{O}_2\cdots \op{O}_p\mid\vvec} = \prod_{k=1}^{\Nvib}\braket{v'_k\mid \op{O}^{(k)}\mid v_k}\]

where \(\op{O}^{(k)}\) is the product, in the original order, of the factors that act on mode \(k\) (the identity if there are none). Single-mode products are evaluated by inserting \(\sum_{v''_k}\ket{v''_k}\bra{v''_k}\) between factors. Each factor changes one quantum number by \(\pm 1\), so a product of \(p\) factors only connects states with \(\sum_k\left|v'_k - v_k\right| \le p\) and \(\sum_k\left(v'_k - v_k\right) \equiv p \pmod 2\).

The reversed action of \(\jop_\pm\) does not change the magnitude of the \(\HRR\) matrix elements, which only involve \(\jop_+^2 + \jop_-^2\), but it fixes the signs below. Using \(\jop_x = \frac{1}{2}\left(\jop_+ + \jop_-\right)\) and \(\jop_y = \frac{1}{2i}\left(\jop_+ - \jop_-\right)\):

\[\begin{split}\braket{J',K'\mid \jop_x\mid J,K} =& \frac{\hbar}{2}\delta_{J',J} \left(C^+_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K+1} + C^-_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K-1}\right)\\ \braket{J',K'\mid \jop_y\mid J,K} =& \frac{i\hbar}{2}\delta_{J',J} \left(C^+_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K+1} - C^-_{J,K}\,\delta_{K',K-1}\right)\\ \braket{J',K'\mid \jop_z\mid J,K} =& \hbar K\,\delta_{J',J}\delta_{K',K}\end{split}\]

Every \(\jop_\alpha\) conserves \(J\), so products are evaluated within a single \(J\) block:

\[\braket{J,K'\mid \jop_\alpha \jop_\beta\mid J,K} = \sum_{K''=K-1}^{K+1}\braket{J,K'\mid \jop_\alpha\mid J,K''} \braket{J,K''\mid \jop_\beta\mid J,K}\]

which is nonzero only for \(\left|K'-K\right| \le 2\).
