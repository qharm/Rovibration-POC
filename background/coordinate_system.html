Coordinate System

The instantaneous geometry of the molecule, \(\tens{r}\), and the minimum energy geometry, \(\tens{r}^0\), will in general be expressed with respect to a somewhat arbitrary space-fixed reference frame. Describing nuclear motion in the space-fixed coordinate system will in general couple the molecule’s internal (vibrational) and external (translational and rotational) degrees of freedom. In practice, it is not possible to completely decouple the internal and external degrees of freedom (e.g., centrifugal distortion caused by rotation will affect the vibrational motion). Our goal is to instead find a coordinate system in which the internal and external degrees of freedom are as decoupled as possible. This coordinate system is known as the Eckart frame and the Eckart conditions provide the mechanism for decoupling the internal and external degrees of freedom. The goal of this section is to re-express quantities in terms of the Eckart frame.

The minimum energy geometry of the molecule is a fixed reference geometry that does not change as the molecule moves. It will follow the Eckart frame as the molecule moves. At each instant we can position the minimum energy geometry in the Eckart frame however we want. We will choose to position it so that its center of mass lies at the origin and the principal axes are aligned with the Eckart frame’s axes. Since the center of mass of the minimum energy geometry is at the origin, we know:

\[\sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}^0_i = \vec{0}.\]

where the use of \(f\) instead of \(r\) is to indicate that the components of the vector are expressed in the Eckart frame. Similarly, we know that in the Eckart frame the inertia tensor of the minimum energy geometry, \(\tens{I}^0\), is diagonal and the elements are given by:

\[I^0_{\alpha\beta} = \delta_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii} \left[\left(\vec{f}^0_i\cdot\vec{f}^0_i\right) - f^0_{i,\alpha}f^0_{i,\beta}\right]\]

Now consider the instantaneous geometry of the molecule. In the Eckart reference frame, the instantaneous center of mass, \(\vec{f}_{\text{COM}}\), of the molecule is given by:

\[\vec{f}_{\text{COM}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}_i} {\sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}}\]

and the instantaneous inertia tensor of the molecule, \(\tens{I}\), is given by:

\[I_{\alpha\beta} = \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii} \left[\left(\vec{f}_i\cdot\vec{f}_i\right)\delta_{\alpha,\beta} - f_{i,\alpha}f_{i,\beta}\right].\]

Since the PES will be Taylor expanded it is convenient to describe the geometry of the molecule in terms of displacements from the minimum energy geometry. Since both the instantaneous and minimum energy geometries move with the Eckart frame, so too will the displacements. To that end, we define \(\vec{\rho}_i\) as the displacement of the \(i\)th atom from \(\vec{f}^0_i\).

The definition of the displacements are then:

\[\vec{\rho}_i = \vec{f}_i - \vec{f}^0_i\]

For convenience we also define mass weighted displacements, \(\tens{P} = \Mvec^{1/2}\tens{\rho}\).

Eckart Conditions

The Eckart frame will be defined in terms of three vectors, \(\hat{e}_x\), \(\hat{e}_y\), and \(\hat{e}_z\), termed the Eckart vectors. Again, for convenience, we define mass-weighted counterparts \(\hat{E}_x\), \(\hat{E}_y\), and \(\hat{E}_z\). Since the Eckart frame moves with the molecule, the Eckart vectors will coincide with the translational vectors of the molecule. Projecting the translations onto the displacements gives:

\[\begin{split}\hat{E}_\alpha\cdot\tens{P} =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii} \left(\hat{e}_\alpha\cdot\vec{\rho}_i\right)\\ =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\rho_{i\alpha}\end{split}\]

To define internal degrees of freedom, we want the displacements to be orthogonal to the Eckart vectors. This leads to the translational Eckart conditions:

\[\sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{\rho}_i = \vec{0}\]

Inserting the definition of the displacements gives:

\[\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\left(\vec{f}_i - \vec{f}^0_i\right) =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}_i - \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}^0_i \\ =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}_i = \vec{0}\end{split}\]

where we have used the fact that the minimum energy geometry is defined so that its center of mass is at the origin. Therefore, the translational Eckart conditions are satisfied if the instantaneous center of mass of the molecule is also at the origin of the Eckart frame.

We can define a basis for the rotational motion of the molecule as \(\vec{s}_\alpha = \hat{e}_\alpha \times \vec{f}^0\). Defining \(\vec{S}_\alpha = \tens{M}^{1/2}\vec{s}_\alpha\), we can project the rotations onto the displacements:

\[\begin{split}\vec{S}_\alpha\cdot\tens{P} =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii} \left(\hat{e}_\alpha\times\vec{f}^0_i\right)\cdot\vec{\rho}_i\\ =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii} \hat{e}_\alpha\cdot\left(\vec{f}^0_i\times\vec{\rho}_i\right)\\ =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\left(\vec{f}^0_i\times\vec{\rho}_i\right)_\alpha\end{split}\]

Yielding the rotational Eckart conditions:

\[\sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}^0_i\times\vec{\rho}_i = \vec{0}\]

Inserting the definition of the displacements gives:

\[\begin{split}\vec{0} =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}^0_i\times\left(\vec{f}_i - \vec{f}^0_i\right) \\ =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}^0_i\times\vec{f}_i - \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}^0_i\times\vec{f}^0_i \\ =& \sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}\vec{f}^0_i\times\vec{f}_i\end{split}\]

In other words, the rotational Eckart conditions are satisfied when the instantaneous geometry of the molecule has no net rotation with respect to the minimum energy geometry.

Conversion from Space-Fixed to Eckart Frame

The Eckart conditions lead to a projection operator:

\[\tens{P}_{\text{Ext}} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{\Natom} M_{ii}} \sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\hat{E}_\alpha\hat{E}_\alpha^T + \sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\frac{\vec{S}_\alpha\vec{S}_\alpha^T} {I^0_{\alpha\alpha}}\]

If \(\bar{H}\) is the mass-weighted Hessian of the PES with respect to displacements from the minimum energy geometry, then the mass-weighted Hessian in the Eckart frame is given by:

\[\bar{\tens{H}}_{\text{Eckart}} = \left(\tens{1} - \tens{P}_{\text{Ext}}\right)^T \bar{\tens{H}}\left(\tens{1} - \tens{P}_{\text{Ext}}\right)\]

Solving:

\[\bar{\tens{H}}_{\text{Eckart}}\bar{\tens{L}} = \bar{\tens{L}}\tens{\Omega}^2\]

gives a diagonal matrix \(\tens{\Omega}\) containing the vibrational frequencies and the mass-weighted vibrational modes, \(\bar{\tens{L}}\). The transformation from the space-fixed frame to the Eckart frame is then given by:

\[\tens{L} = \tens{M}\bar{\tens{L}}\]
